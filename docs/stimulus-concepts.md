# Normal questions, unreasonable objects

**Design brief — 20 October 2026.** All fifteen pools were published on 27 October 2026 as grids C01–C15, numbered as in the table below; most use two normal, two bizarre, two debatable and three non-member images. Items without a usable image are listed in the [source registry](../stimuli/SOURCES.md), and every image is in the [collection gallery](../stimuli/GALLERY.md).

The Department asks completely ordinary CAPTCHA questions. Images mix normal category members, recognisable but **impossible or profoundly absurd** category members, and genuine category-boundary cases. The question is the straight man; the image is the joke. Not merely inconvenient product design, novelty animal-shaped lamps or accidental AI image artefacts. A viewer should recognise the object immediately, spot a ridiculous physical contradiction, and still find the photograph strangely believable. All new grids use **“Select all…”** and allow multiple selections.

## Visual generation guidance

Use **realistic, object-appropriate natural environments** rather than neutral studio backgrounds by default. Keep the surrounding world unremarkable even when the object is absurd. **Do not make the set visually monotonous:** vary natural object colours, material finishes, facing directions and photographed views (side, front-left/right three-quarter, modestly elevated) across all four stimulus classes. Maintain comparable legibility, soft lighting, object prominence and background clutter; protect the visibility of the bizarre feature. The [C01 shot plan](stimuli.md#c01--bicycle-pool-colour-angle-and-setting-plan) gives a concrete twelve-image example. Avoid additional category members in the background.

The full rules, **C01 colour/viewpoint/background plan**, generation-prompt pattern, current 512 px pipeline specifications and manual review checklist are in [Stimulus photography and background guidelines](stimuli.md#stimulus-photography-and-background-guidelines). Apply these to all new candidate images before publication.

## Candidate image pools (12 examples per theme)

Each row provides a **pool of 12 possible images**: **three member-normal, three member-bizarre, three member-debatable and three member-non**. The columns describe *candidate ideas*, not twelve simultaneously displayed tiles. The current game still displays **nine images per 3 × 3 grid**. A sensible starting selection is two normal members, two bizarre members, three debatable candidates and two non-members; other compositions can be tested deliberately. Choose a subset **before rendering and publishing** each playable grid. The game must not silently truncate or randomly sample a 12-item source set as though it were a nine-image grid.

The ordinary player-facing question is **“Select all images containing a [theme].”** Use natural singular wording in the actual grid, such as “Select all images containing a bicycle” or “… a musical instrument.” Keep the same instruction for controlled variants. These roles are **initial design judgements, not verified ground truth**: even radically bizarre items can clearly belong to the category, and genuinely debatable cases are not marked wrong. Rate humour and visual recognisability independently from category-membership uncertainty, then pilot the categories before finalising roles. Context and framing follow the [photography guidelines](stimuli.md#stimulus-photography-and-background-guidelines).

**Computers: a screen is not a computer** (owner's correction, 27 October 2026). A monitor or other screen on its own never counts as a computer, so impossible computers must show the computer itself: a laptop, a tablet or a tower. A standalone monitor is a non-member, and an all-in-one computer, whose computer is hidden inside the display, is treated as debatable rather than as a clear target.

All pictures must have documented provenance and rights-cleared assets. These are **original creative briefs**, not claims that the exact objects already exist or permission to reproduce specific artworks. Avoid gratuitous gore, injury, copied characters or copyrighted stills.

### Editorial filter for bizarre and debatable ideas

- **Bizarre ≠ novelty design.** An uncomfortable chair, a tall hat, a horse-shaped lamp, an oddly coloured product or an impractical object alone does **not** meet the standard. Include an unmistakable impossibility, deliberate category-specific surreal anatomy, inverted physical logic or recognisable visual punchline while the underlying category stays obvious.
- **Debatable ≠ strange.** A square-wheeled bicycle is still a bicycle. A living horse with pedals, a human acting as a chair or a motorised bathtub raises a genuine dispute about category membership. Look for conflict between **appearance, identity, original purpose and current function**.
- **One-second, no-caption test.** At actual game-tile size, the object and its main joke should be comprehensible without reading a description or finding tiny details. An elaborate text prompt is not proof of visual legibility.
- **Everyday scene, impossible event.** Use a quiet, naturally plausible background with consistent lighting, perspective and scale. People or animals can appear when essential to a gag, despite the usual preference for scenes without people.
- **Vary the mechanisms.** Spread humour across impossible biomechanics, absurd scale, hybrid identity, object self-action, recursion and literalised phrases. A whole collection of objects on legs quickly becomes predictable.
- **Review after generation.** “Bizarre” and “debatable” are provisional design hypotheses. Obtain independent ratings of category membership and perceived absurdity; reclassify or discard images that are merely unusual, trivial, impossible to read or indistinguishable from accidental model defects.

The photographic aspiration is the **realistic depiction of impossible events**, as in [Erik Johansson's approach](https://www.erikjo.com/about). [*The Uncomfortable*](https://www.theuncomfortable.com/about/) illustrates a distinct approach based on deliberate impracticality; inconvenient products alone do not meet the game's new humour threshold. These are **conceptual references, not artwork to reproduce**.

## Judgement mechanics

A grid is **not** a single-answer puzzle. It has three tile classes: `target` (clear member), `distractor` (clear non-member) and `borderline` (genuinely defensible either way). Only clear members and clear non-members contribute to the “verified” count. A borderline image is recorded as a player's category judgement whether selected or not; never call it wrong or display a red “Not a match” badge. Preserve the selection order and response time. The fictional Department may be disappointed by a horse being classified as a bicycle, but cannot claim an objective verdict on that case.

Example feedback: “The square-wheeled bicycle has been recognised. Its roadworthiness has not.” / “Your classification of the horse is under administrative review.” / “No policy currently exists for pedal-powered office furniture.”

## Design and manipulation principles

- **No funny instructions:** the prompts remain ordinary, literally interpretable and unchanged across image variants.
- **Blend in:** use a consistent realistic photographic look, scale, crop and lighting. A bizarre image should depict a recognisable category member with a clearly visible impossible feature; debatable images should instead raise a real category-boundary question.
- **Separate axes:** visual resemblance to the category, functional resemblance, perceived bizarreness, and category-membership confidence are conceptually distinct. Rate them independently, rather than treating “AI weirdness” as one uniform feature.
- **Systematic comparisons:** construct alternate grids with the same clear members, number of borderline cases, instruction, image quality and appropriately balanced tile positions. Vary one target image property deliberately; do not assume changes from one arbitrary image to another constitute an experiment.
- **Pilot:** obtain human ratings of recognisability and category ambiguity, then reclassify or discard disputed “clear” cases. Extreme grotesquerie and gratuitous shock are not needed.
- **Source of truth:** store individual prompts, seeds, image hashes, labels and decisions in the existing stimulus pipeline. Generated images or edits must be manually reviewed before marking a grid `published`.
- **No V2 work:** the ordinary game does not use a webcam or transmit behavioural data.

## Migration from the current library

The current G01 bicycle grid remains playable until replacement assets exist. The 12-item pools above are planning material, not changes to the nine-tile live-game format. The unpublished G02–G10 drafts in `tools/stimuli/source.json` implement the earlier “one objectively correct target per odd task” concept; **do not publish those drafts unchanged**. Replace them with the new normal-instruction/multiple-category-member sets progressively. Once enough are published, show five distinct categories in a session instead of repeating the same bicycle grid. Do not invent image availability or mark a proposed concept as a rendered asset.
